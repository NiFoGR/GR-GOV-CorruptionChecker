Methodology

← Back to the flags

A flag is a question, not an accusation. This site applies statistical rules to public records. A flagged award is one that fits a pattern which can indicate a problem — and very often does not. Most flagged items have ordinary explanations: routine purchasing, an urgent repair, a genuinely small local market with one credible supplier. Nothing here is a finding of wrongdoing by any person or company.

Where the data comes from

Every spending decision by a Greek public body must be published on Διαύγεια (Diavgeia) to be legally valid. We read its open API — no scraping, no private data — and use decision type Δ.1, ΑΝΑΘΕΣΗ ΕΡΓΩΝ / ΠΡΟΜΗΘΕΙΩΝ / ΥΠΗΡΕΣΙΩΝ / ΜΕΛΕΤΩΝ (awards of works, supplies, services and studies). Each record supplies the awarding body, the supplier and their tax number, the amount, and a CPV product code. Diavgeia data is published under CC-BY.

The scan runs daily and covers a rolling recent window. Every figure on this site links back to the original document so you can verify it directly.

The rules

This buyer's prices cluster below the limit

Greek law (art. 118, N.4412/2016, as amended by N.4782/2021) lets a public body award a contract directly, without a tender, up to €30,000 excluding VAT. Above that, competition is required.

A single award at €29,500 is unremarkable — plenty of real work costs that. What is not unremarkable is a body where a quarter or more of all awards land in the last 10% below the ceiling, at three times or more the national rate. That is the ceiling setting the price rather than the work setting it. We compute the national rate from the same scan rather than assuming a figure, so the comparison moves with the data.

Priced just under the direct-award limit

Individual awards within 3% of a ceiling. Weak on its own, which is why it is medium severity and confined to the tightest band — it is context for the buyer-level rule above, not a finding in itself.

Repeat awards that together pass the limit

Splitting one requirement into several smaller awards to stay under the tender threshold (κατάτμηση) is explicitly prohibited. We group awards by buyer, supplier and CPV class within a 90-day window, and flag groups where each award sits below the ceiling but the total exceeds it.

One supplier dominates this buyer's awards

We flag a supplier holding at least five awards from one body and at least half of that body's total award value in the period. Concentration is not misconduct — small municipalities often have few real suppliers — but it is where capture, if present, becomes visible.

Also an exactly round amount

Costed quotes rarely land on an exact multiple of €1,000 — but public budgets are written in round figures, so on its own this matches a large share of all awards and tells you nothing. We therefore only report it for awards another rule has already flagged, as a corroborating detail rather than a finding.

Known limitations

  • VAT is ambiguous. The threshold in law is set excluding VAT, but Diavgeia's awardAmount does not reliably state whether the figure it carries includes VAT. We test both the €30,000 and the VAT-inclusive €37,200 band and label which one matched. Some flags will therefore be measuring against the wrong band.
  • Rolling window, not history. The API applies a default six-month submission window that silently narrows older date queries, so this covers recent activity rather than multi-year trends.
  • Awards only. We currently read award decisions, not payments, contract amendments, or tender notices. An award that was later cancelled or reduced still appears here.
  • No supplier ownership data. Without the ΓΕΜΗ business registry we cannot see incorporation dates, directors, or shared addresses between bidders — the signals that distinguish a coincidence from a pattern.
  • Rules are untuned. The thresholds here are reasoned starting points, not values calibrated against confirmed cases. Expect false positives.

Privacy

We report awards to legal persons — companies and organisations. Where a record names a natural person, that is a sole trader acting in a commercial capacity, published by the State as a condition of the award's validity. We do not republish grants, benefits, or salaries paid to individuals, and we do not build profiles of named officials.

Corrections and right of reply

If you are named here and believe something is wrong or misleading, we want to fix it. Open an issue on the project's GitHub with the ΑΔΑ of the decision. Corrections are applied to the rules, not just the page, so the same error stops recurring. A response explaining a flagged award will be published next to it on request.

Open source

Every rule is a readable function in scripts/analyze.py. If you think a rule is wrong, you can read it, run it, and send a fix.
